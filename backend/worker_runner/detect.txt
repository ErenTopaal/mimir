## Avalanche-specific vulnerability patterns

In addition to standard Solidity vulnerability classes, pay special attention to the following patterns that are unique to or especially relevant on Avalanche:

- **Avalanche Warp Messaging (AWM) validation issues** — Verify that contracts consuming AWM messages properly validate the source chain ID and sender address. Failure to check the originating chain or sender authorization can allow forged cross-chain messages.
- **Cross-subnet communication safety** — Contracts that interact across Avalanche subnets must enforce strict trust boundaries. Look for trust boundary violations where a contract blindly trusts data originating from a different subnet without independent verification.
- **Precompile abuse** — Avalanche networks expose custom precompiled contracts (e.g., for native minting, fee configuration, contract deployer allow lists, and reward managers). Audit any interaction with these precompiles for unexpected behavior, access-control bypasses, or reentrancy through precompile callbacks.
- **Subnet bridge trust assumptions** — Cross-chain asset transfers between subnets or between C-Chain and subnets must validate token mappings, amounts, and finality. Improper validation of bridge messages can lead to infinite minting, double-spending, or asset theft.
- **C-Chain vs Subnet execution differences** — Gas costs, block timing, and opcode availability can differ between C-Chain and custom subnets. Identify any hardcoded gas assumptions, block-time-dependent logic, or reliance on opcodes that may not behave identically across chain configurations.
